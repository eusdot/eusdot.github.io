I work at the intersection of knowledge, technology, and organisational change. My focus is on translating complex challenges — scientific, technological, human — into solutions that work in practice, at scale, and over time.

## What I do
Currently acting as Director and Head of Collective Knowledge at Takeda, a global biopharmaceutical company, I lead the development of knowledge and data capabilities across a global organisation — building the systems, practices, and culture that make knowledge accessible, reusable, and decision-relevant. Prior to this, I led digital transformation across the pharmaceutical sciences organisation, building a digital lab environment and translating emerging technologies into operational reality in a regulated environment.

A consistent thread runs through both roles: the most consequential work happens at the boundary between the technical and the organisational — between what a system is capable of and what an organisation is genuinely prepared to absorb.

## Background
I trained as a scientist — PhD and MSc in biotechnology from Graz University of Technology and IMC University of Applied Sciences Krems, with research stays in Norway, Belgium, and Denmark. My doctoral work was in enzyme technology and biocatalysis, resulting in seven first-author publications and a patent. That scientific formation established working habits that have proved durable: reasoning from evidence, maintaining productive uncertainty, and resisting conclusions that arrive before the complexity has been properly examined.

The move from bench science to transformation work was intentional. Organisations, like biological systems, are complex and adaptive. They do not respond to interventions predictably, and they cannot be engineered into new states by instruction alone. That recognition has shaped how I approach every transformation program since.

## How I think
Reading cuts across leadership, learning science, systems thinking, knowledge management, and the social dimensions of technology. The most generative thinking tends to happen at the edges of disciplines — and the frameworks brought to a problem largely determine what becomes visible within it.

I write about these ideas and occasionally speak about them publicly as serious engagement with these questions is how thinking advances.

## Beyond work
I live in Vienna with my family. I run, spend time outdoors, and read widely — with a particular interest in how people learn, how organisations change, and how individuals navigate complexity across the full range of their lives. These are questions I find as compelling away from work as within it.
